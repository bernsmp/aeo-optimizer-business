## **SECTION 1: CORE VOICE CHARACTERISTICS**

### **The Three Non-Negotiables**

These override all other considerations:

1. **PRACTICAL DIRECTNESS**
   - Every piece of advice must be immediately actionable
   - No theory without application
   - Always answer: "What should they do?"
   - **Rule:** If it can't be implemented today, it's not Louie's voice

2. **EXPERIENCE-BASED AUTHORITY**
   - Ground everything in real experience (22 years running a business, 50+ years in sales)
   - Use specific numbers, dates, and concrete examples
   - Reference actual situations: "I once stepped in to help a sales team..."
   - **Rule:** Never make claims without backing them with experience

3. **EMPATHETIC LEADERSHIP**
   - Acknowledge the struggle first ("I've been where you are")
   - Then provide the solution
   - Balance firmness with understanding
   - **Rule:** Challenge with compassion, not criticism

## **SECTION 2: SENTENCE STRUCTURE & RHYTHM**

### **Louie's Sentence Patterns**

**Average Sentence Length:** 12-18 words (slightly longer than Jay Abraham, but still direct)

**Pattern Mix:**
- **Short punches:** 3-8 words for emphasis ("Wait, what??" "It did not.")
- **Medium explanations:** 12-18 words for clarity
- **Longer context:** 20-25 words when telling a story or explaining a framework

**Example from Louie's writing:**
> "I got a note about a salesperson doing so well, they carved out another role for him to sell a different, struggling product within the company. It also came with a new manager. Friction quickly came between the two and the rep was put on a PIP. Here's the problem:"

**Rhythm Pattern:**
- Start with story/context (medium sentence)
- Add detail (short sentence)
- Build tension (medium sentence)
- Deliver insight ("Here's the problem:")

### **Paragraph Structure**

**Average Paragraph Length:** 2-4 sentences

**Pattern:**
- **Opening:** Set context or state the problem
- **Development:** Add detail or explain
- **Conclusion:** Deliver the insight or action step

**Example:**
> "100 calls a day. Almost no sales. Here's why most 'busy' salespeople never hit their numbers. Ever seen someone glued to the phone, rattling off calls, looking like a machine? I have."

## **SECTION 3: TONE & STYLE MARKERS**

### **Tone Characteristics**

**1. Direct but Not Harsh**
- ✅ "Sales leaders, save yourself the headaches and just put it in writing."
- ❌ "You're doing it wrong."
- ✅ "Founders, stop guessing and stressing."
- ❌ "You're incompetent."

**2. Conversational Authority**
- Uses "I" frequently to establish credibility
- Shares personal experiences ("I ran a small business for 22 years...")
- Admits mistakes ("I thought I was being productive working 12 hours per day. I was not.")

**3. Practical Urgency**
- Creates urgency without being pushy
- "EVERYONE will be better off because you did."
- "Do this. Now."
- "Start today."

### **Common Phrases & Patterns**

**Opening Patterns:**
- "Here's why..."
- "Here's what I did..."
- "Here's the problem:"
- "I got a note about..."
- "I once..."
- "Founders, [direct address]"

**Transition Patterns:**
- "Here's what I did (and so should you if you're leading a sales team):"
- "Here's how to start:"
- "Here's 5 [things/ways/steps]:"
- "Here's why this works:"

**Closing Patterns:**
- "EVERYONE will be better off because you did."
- "What would you add?"
- "What do you think?"
- "Agree?"
- "Keep going!"

**Emphasis Patterns:**
- ALL CAPS for critical points: "EVERYONE", "ALWAYS"
- Short standalone sentences: "It did not." "Wait, what??"
- Repetition for emphasis: "Do what you said you would do."

## **SECTION 4: AUDIENCE ADDRESS**

### **Primary Audiences**

**1. Founders (Most Common)**
- Direct address: "Founders, [instruction]"
- Acknowledges their unique challenges
- Emphasizes they have more to lose
- Example: "Bootstrapped Founders, you have more to lose than most."

**2. Sales Leaders / Sales Managers**
- Direct address: "Sales Leaders, [instruction]"
- Focuses on team management
- Emphasizes accountability and systems
- Example: "Sales Leaders, save yourself the headaches and just put it in writing."

**3. Salespeople**
- Direct address: "Salespeople, [instruction]"
- Focuses on individual performance
- Emphasizes skills and mindset
- Example: "Salespeople – List three additional responses to, 'We're happy with what we already have.' and keep them ready."

### **Address Patterns**

**Direct "You" Usage:**
- High frequency: 15-25 instances per 100 words
- Always specific: "If you're leading a sales team..." not "If one is leading..."
- Mix of "you" and "your" for variety

**Collective Address:**
- "Founders" (plural) when addressing the group
- "Your team" when discussing management
- "Your salespeople" when talking to leaders

## **SECTION 6: STORYTELLING PATTERNS**

### **Story Structure**

**1. The Problem Story**
- Opens with a surprising situation
- Reveals the problem
- Explains the solution
- Delivers the lesson

**Example:**
> "135% of Quota and put on a PIP. Wait, what?? I got a note about a salesperson doing so well, they carved out another role for him to sell a different, struggling product within the company. It also came with a new manager. Friction quickly came between the two and the rep was put on a PIP. Here's the problem: He was never given a job description..."

**2. The Personal Experience Story**
- Starts with "I once..." or "I was..."
- Includes specific details (location, time, context)
- Shows the mistake or learning
- Connects to broader principle

**Example:**
> "23 years ago, I was in California for an HP conference. I got up early, went to the hotel gym, got on the treadmill, and was shocked by what I saw on the TV. It was the aftermath of two jets flying into the World Trade Center towers..."

**3. The Case Study Story**
- Names the situation
- Describes what happened
- Shows the result
- Extracts the principle

**Example:**
> "I once stepped in to help a sales team where one rep was making over 100 calls daily. Results? Almost zero. And the customer buying cycle was under 30 days. There was no reason not to close business..."

### **Story Elements**

**Always Include:**
- Specific context (when, where, who)
- The problem or challenge
- What was done
- The outcome (with numbers if possible)
- The lesson

**Never Include:**
- Vague references ("a company I worked with...")
- Unverifiable claims
- Stories without a clear lesson

## **SECTION 7: LIST & FRAMEWORK PATTERNS**

### **Numbered Lists**

**Common Patterns:**
- "Here's 5 [things/ways/steps]:"
- "5 pieces of advice that will accelerate your success"
- "12 Things I Wish I Knew About Sales Management at Age 30"
- "15 qualities that make a great salesperson"

**List Structure:**
- Number + descriptive title
- Each item is actionable
- Often includes "What would you add?" at the end

**Example:**
> "5 sales tips every founder needs to teach their sales team:
> 1. Like. Trust. Buy. It always goes in this order. Always.
> 2. The best salespeople I've known are the most humble. They let their results speak for themselves.
> 3. If you're not working within a sales process, you have to start over every day. Consistency brings mastery.
> 4. The Gatekeeper is not your enemy. Make them a friend and watch your sales grow.
> 5. Do what you said you would do. It's the fastest way to build trust. Trust brings sales."

### **Framework Presentation**

**Louie's Frameworks Are:**
- Practical (not theoretical)
- Step-by-step (clear sequence)
- Actionable (can be implemented immediately)
- Proven (backed by experience)

**Framework Structure:**
1. Name the framework
2. Explain why it matters
3. List the components/steps
4. Provide examples
5. Show the result

**Example Framework:**
> "The one-to-one shouldn't last more than 30 to 45 minutes. Here's some topics I suggest discussing, and they include:
> - What's working?
> - What's not working?
> - How can I do better?
> - What could our company be doing better?
> - Do you have all the tools you need to be successful?
> - How would you evaluate your performance?
> - Do you have any new goals?
> - Are there any personal issues you want to discuss?"

## **SECTION 8: QUESTION USAGE**

### **Types of Questions**

**1. Engagement Questions (End of Posts)**
- "What would you add?"
- "What do you think?"
- "Agree?"
- "What's your approach?"
- "How do you handle this?"
- "What would you have done?"

**2. Rhetorical Questions (Rare, But Used)**
- "Wait, what??"
- "Here's why most 'busy' salespeople never hit their numbers. Ever seen someone glued to the phone, rattling off calls, looking like a machine?"

**3. Direct Questions (To Make a Point)**
- "Do you have enough resilience? Find out here."
- "Are you ready for calm within your sales team?"

### **Question Patterns**

**Avoid:**
- Overuse of rhetorical questions
- Questions without answers
- Vague questions ("Have you ever wondered...?")

**Use:**
- Questions that invite engagement
- Questions that set up the answer
- Questions that challenge thinking

## **SECTION 9: ACTIVE VS. PASSIVE VOICE**

### **Voice Ratio**

**Target:** 85-95% active voice, 5-15% passive voice

**Active Voice Examples:**
- ✅ "I once stepped in to help a sales team..."
- ✅ "I got a note about a salesperson..."
- ✅ "I ran a small business for 22 years..."
- ✅ "Sales leaders, save yourself the headaches..."

**Passive Voice (Used Sparingly):**
- ✅ "He was never given a job description..." (emphasizes the problem)
- ✅ "The rep was put on a PIP..." (focuses on the action taken)

**Never Use Passive:**
- ❌ "It was decided that..."
- ❌ "Mistakes were made..."
- ❌ "The situation was handled..."

## **SECTION 16: VOICE SCORING SYSTEM**

### **Voice Consistency Score (0-100)**

**Scoring Breakdown:**

**Directness (25 points):**
- 20-25: All advice is immediately actionable, no theory without application
- 15-19: Mostly actionable, some theoretical concepts
- 10-14: Mix of actionable and theoretical
- 0-9: Mostly theoretical, little actionable content

**Specificity (25 points):**
- 20-25: 5+ specific numbers per 100 words, no generic terms
- 15-19: 3-4 specific numbers per 100 words, few generic terms
- 10-14: 1-2 specific numbers per 100 words, some generic terms
- 0-9: Few or no specific numbers, many generic terms

**Experience-Based Authority (20 points):**
- 16-20: Multiple specific examples, stories, or case studies
- 12-15: Some examples or stories
- 8-11: Few examples, mostly general statements
- 0-7: No examples or stories, all general statements

**Tone & Empathy (15 points):**
- 12-15: Perfect balance of firmness and understanding
- 9-11: Good balance, slightly off
- 6-8: Too harsh or too soft
- 0-5: Tone doesn't match Louie's voice

**Sentence Structure (10 points):**
- 8-10: Perfect mix of short and medium sentences, good rhythm
- 6-7: Good mix, minor rhythm issues
- 4-5: Monotonous or choppy
- 0-3: Poor sentence structure

**Active Voice (5 points):**
- 4-5: 85%+ active voice
- 3: 70-84% active voice
- 1-2: 50-69% active voice
- 0: <50% active voice

### **Target Score: 85+**

**Score Interpretation:**
- **90-100:** Excellent - Publication ready
- **85-89:** Good - Minor tweaks needed
- **75-84:** Fair - Needs revision
- **60-74:** Poor - Major revision needed
- **<60:** Fail - Complete rewrite needed

## **SECTION 17: COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID**

### **Voice Breakers**

**1. Being Too Academic**
- ❌ "Research indicates that..."
- ✅ "I've found that..."

**2. Being Too Soft**
- ❌ "You might want to consider..."
- ✅ "Do this."

**3. Being Too Harsh**
- ❌ "You're doing it wrong."
- ✅ "Here's a better way..."

**4. Being Vague**
- ❌ "Many companies struggle with..."
- ✅ "25% of new salespeople fail within the first six months."

**5. Missing the Story**
- ❌ "Accountability is important."
- ✅ "I got a note about a salesperson doing so well, they carved out another role for him... Here's the problem: He was never given a job description..."

**6. Overusing Questions**
- ❌ "Have you ever wondered why sales teams struggle? What if there was a better way? Could this be the solution?"
- ✅ "Here's why most 'busy' salespeople never hit their numbers."

**7. Using Business Jargon**
- ❌ "Leverage your sales team to optimize revenue."
- ✅ "Use your sales team to increase revenue."

**8. Missing the "I"**
- ❌ "It's important to have written accountabilities."
- ✅ "I've found that written accountabilities eliminate surprises."

## **SECTION 19: EXAMPLES OF PERFECT VOICE**

### **Example 1: Problem-Solution Post**

> "135% of Quota and put on a PIP.
> 
> Wait, what??
> 
> I got a note about a salesperson doing so well, they carved out another role for him to sell a different, struggling product within the company.
> 
> It also came with a new manager.
> 
> Friction quickly came between the two and the rep was put on a PIP. Here's the problem:
> 
> He was never given a job description or a written list of what he was accountable for, or actions he was supposed to take during his work activities.
> 
> Every job needs to have a written list of accountabilities, including a new job at the same company. There should never be any surprises as to what is expected and not expected of a salesperson.
> 
> If it's spelled out and the salesperson doesn't comply, a PIP is understandable.
> 
> If it's not spelled completely out and it's just random, subjective attitude, it's management's fault.
> 
> Sales leaders, save yourself the headaches and just put it in writing.
> 
> EVERYONE will be better off because you did."

**Why This Works:**
- ✅ Surprising hook
- ✅ Specific story
- ✅ Clear problem identification
- ✅ Direct solution
- ✅ Actionable advice
- ✅ Strong closing

### **Example 2: Framework Post**

> "12 Things I Wish I Knew About Sales Management at Age 30
> 
> And it's not, 'Just make more calls!!'
> 
> 1. Have a one-on-one with each person in the sales department every month.
> 
> 2. Do a pipeline review with Account Executives (AE's) once per week.
> 
> 3. Have a 15-minute coaching session just for outbound Business Development Reps (BDR's) every morning and listen to successful calls (Gold Calls). Choose calls where an appointment was set, demo scheduled, etc. Reinforce positive behavior and results.
> 
> 4. Keep your funnel stages simple. Four at most leading to an Opportunity. Four at most post initial meeting or demo. Consistency will bring a better forecast.
> 
> 5. Make sure you receive an automated notification when an Opportunity is created.
> 
> 6. Hold sales training every business day for 33 minutes. The team should always be learning and thinking about sales.
> 
> 7. Make sure each sales job function has a Position Contract that is signed by the salesperson. You can use the some of the job posting verbiage used to attract the candidate. It should include:
> - A job summary
> - Duties and responsibilities
> - What's required from them to be successful in that position
> - What a typical day should look like
> - Items for which they will be held accountable – Leave no doubts
> 
> 8. Everyone, including you, should have a self-improvement plan that includes self-care.
> 
> 9. Develop a series of Key Performance Indicators (KPI's) that are relevant and essential to the success of the sales team and really move the needle. This includes KPI's for BDR's, AE's, and Sales Managers.
> 
> 10. Always be interviewing applicants. Even if you have no openings. It will build a talent pool that can be drawn upon when there is an opening. It also keeps interviewing skills sharp.
> 
> 11. Develop an onboarding system that gets new salespeople productive as soon as possible.
> 
> 12. Lead by example. Show the team how to accomplish the goal. However, the job is to teach your sales team how to fish. Not make them a fish dinner every night.
> 
> What would you add?
> 
> Your job is to teach your sales team how to fish.
> Not make them a fish dinner every night."

**Why This Works:**
- ✅ Clear framework (numbered list)
- ✅ Specific, actionable items
- ✅ Includes timeframes and details
- ✅ Ends with engagement question
- ✅ Memorable closing line

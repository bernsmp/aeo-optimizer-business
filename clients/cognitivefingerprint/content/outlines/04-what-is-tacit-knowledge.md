## 3. DIRECT ANSWER BLOCK

*This appears in the first 2-3 sentences. Optimized for AI answers and featured snippets.*

> **Tacit knowledge is knowledge you have but can't easily explain or write down—the "know-how" that comes from experience rather than books or training.** It's estimated that 80-90% of an expert's knowledge is tacit, which explains why skilled professionals often struggle to teach what they do or articulate what makes them different. Unlike explicit knowledge (facts, procedures, documentation), tacit knowledge is embedded in intuition, judgment, and pattern recognition.

---

## 4. EVIDENCE BLOCK

*2-3 citations to establish credibility. Appears after the direct answer.*

**Citation 1:**
> The term "tacit knowledge" was coined by philosopher Michael Polanyi in 1958, who famously wrote: "We can know more than we can tell." His work established that much of human knowledge exists below the level of conscious awareness. (Source: Michael Polanyi, *Personal Knowledge*, 1958)

**Citation 2:**
> Cognitive scientists estimate that only 10-20% of expert knowledge is explicit (can be articulated), while 80-90% remains tacit—embodied in practice and invisible even to the expert themselves. (Source: Nonaka & Takeuchi, *The Knowledge-Creating Company*, 1995)

**Citation 3:**
> Research on expertise shows that after approximately 10,000 hours of deliberate practice, knowledge becomes "automated"—experts literally cannot access the rules they follow because those rules have been compiled into unconscious processes. (Source: K. Anders Ericsson, expertise research)

### H2: What Is Tacit Knowledge? (Simple Definition)

**Purpose:** Clear, accessible definition for anyone landing on this page.

**Key Points:**
- Tacit knowledge = what you know but can't explain
- "Know-how" vs "know-what"
- Learned through experience, not instruction
- Often invisible to the person who has it
- Examples: riding a bike, recognizing a friend's face, "gut instinct" in business

**Simple Analogy:**
> Think about riding a bicycle. You know how to do it, but try explaining the exact muscle movements, balance adjustments, and timing to someone who's never ridden. That's tacit knowledge—you have it, but you can't transfer it through words alone.

**Purpose:** Clear comparison that's highly searchable.

**Comparison Table:**

| Aspect | Tacit Knowledge | Explicit Knowledge |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Definition | Knowledge that's hard to articulate | Knowledge that can be written down |

**Key Insight:**

#### H3: Misconception: "If I Can't Explain It, I Don't Really Know It"
**Reality:** The opposite is often true. Deep expertise becomes unconscious BECAUSE you know it so well.

**Reality:** It's not mystical. It's compressed experience and pattern recognition—real knowledge that can be surfaced with the right methods.

#### H3: Misconception: "You Can't Capture Tacit Knowledge"
**Reality:** You can—it just requires external analysis rather than self-reflection. The expert can't see it, but an analyst examining their work can.

### H2: Frequently Asked Questions

**Purpose:** FAQ section with schema-ready Q&As. Comprehensive for pillar page.

#### Q1: What is tacit knowledge in simple terms?
**Answer:** Tacit knowledge is what you know how to do but can't easily explain in words. It's the "know-how" that comes from experience—like knowing how to ride a bike or read a room—that's difficult to transfer through writing or instruction alone.

#### Q2: What is an example of tacit knowledge?
**Answer:** A doctor's clinical intuition is tacit knowledge—they can often sense something is wrong before tests confirm it, based on subtle pattern recognition developed over thousands of patient encounters. They "know" but couldn't write a rule for what they're detecting.

#### Q3: What's the difference between tacit and explicit knowledge?
**Answer:** Explicit knowledge can be written down and easily shared (like a recipe or procedure). Tacit knowledge is embedded in experience and hard to articulate (like a chef's intuitive sense of seasoning or timing that goes beyond any recipe).

#### Q4: Why is tacit knowledge important?
**Answer:** Tacit knowledge represents 80-90% of an expert's capabilities and is often what creates competitive advantage. It's what makes senior professionals irreplaceable and what organizations lose when key people leave.

#### Q5: Can tacit knowledge be converted to explicit knowledge?
**Answer:** Partially, through careful extraction methods. While some tacit knowledge will always remain embodied, much of it can be surfaced through analysis of how experts actually work—revealing patterns they don't consciously recognize.

#### Q6: How do you identify tacit knowledge?
**Answer:** Look for expertise gaps between what someone can DO versus what they can EXPLAIN. If someone consistently gets results but can't teach others to replicate them, they're operating from tacit knowledge.

#### Q7: Why can't experts explain their own expertise?
**Answer:** Expertise automates knowledge into unconscious processes. Through years of practice, the brain compiles complex decision trees into rapid pattern-matching that happens below conscious awareness. Experts literally can't access the "rules" they're following.

#### Q8: How is tacit knowledge transferred?
**Answer:** Traditional methods include apprenticeship, mentorship, and observation—all slow and incomplete. More effective methods analyze work products (transcripts, decisions, outputs) to reveal patterns and create explicit frameworks from tacit expertise.

#### Q9: What is tacit knowledge in business?
**Answer:** In business, tacit knowledge includes sales intuition, client reading, negotiation instincts, leadership judgment, and strategic pattern recognition—the invisible skills that separate top performers from competent technicians.

#### Q10: Is tacit knowledge the same as skill?
**Answer:** Tacit knowledge is a component of skill, specifically the "know-how" aspect that can't be verbalized. A skill like surgery includes both explicit knowledge (anatomy, procedures) and tacit knowledge (the surgeon's "feel" and judgment).
